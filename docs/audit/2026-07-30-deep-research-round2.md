## R2.2 In arXiv v1, the HMM's filter was FIXED to Bulla — CV-over-k is a v3 invention

arXiv v1 of 2402.05272 (different data, different protocol) states of its HMM
benchmark:

> "the hidden state sequence generated by the HMM has undergone the median
> filtering procedure as implemented in Bulla et al., (2011); without this
> filtering, the performance is worse."

No cross-validation over the window; the filter is Bulla's own, k = 6. The
CV-over-k mechanism — the very thing whose candidate set is unpublished and
whose k=0 selections drive our excess — first appears in v3. So there is no
earlier version of the paper from which the candidate set could be recovered:
v1 had no set to publish, v3 has one and does not publish it.

v1's footnote 5 also fixes the restart convention: "Our HMM implementation
using the Baum-Welch algorithm is also initialized ten times for improved
performance." — consistent with v3's [line 371] "we execute the algorithm ten times from"
different k-means++-derived initial values, and with our 10-seed protocol.

(For the record, v1's JM protocol also differs from v3's: 2000-day lookback,
biannual refit, optimal λ = 100.0 — the protocol drifted across versions, which
is one more reason nothing numeric from v1 may be imported into a v3 claim.)

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v1, fetched and read 2026-07-30.

## R2.3 The author's own 2023 codebase: structurally identical HMM, five different constants

Yizhan Shu's `continuous-jump-model` repository (github.com/Yizhan-Oliver-Shu/
continuous-jump-model, last pushed 2023-05-31 — the simulation codebase
predating the v3 paper) contains a `GaussianHMM_model` wrapper
(`regime/jump.py`, class at line 505) whose structure matches our sealed
implementation exactly:

- hmmlearn `GaussianHMM`, `init_params="sct"` (ours: `"stc"`, the same set);
- means seeded from k-means++, 10 restarts, keep the highest log-likelihood
  (ours: 10 seeds, same rule);
- decoding via `.predict()` — full-window Viterbi hard assignment; smoothed
  posteriors stored but unused for labels. No filtered/one-step-ahead code
  anywhere in the repo (ours: Viterbi, terminal state);
- **no median filter, no smoothing-window candidate set, no CV code anywhere
  in the repository** — the v3 machinery postdates it.

The differences are five estimation constants
(`notebooks/simulation/1-model-fitting-2-state-HMM.py` line 61):

| constant | author's simulation code | ours (sealed v9.4) | hmmlearn default |
|---|---|---|---|
| min_covar | 1e-6 | 1e-3 | 1e-3 |
| covars_prior | 1e-6 | 0.0 | 1e-2 |
| transmat_prior | 1 + 1e-5 | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| n_iter | 500 | 1000 | 10 |
| tol | 1e-4 | 1e-6 (symmetric strict gate) | 1e-2 |

Both columns are near-MLE: the priors on both sides are zero or ~1e-6-scale
pseudo-counts, and our convergence gate is the stricter one. A priori these
should converge to the same local optima given identical data and
initialization. This is recorded as a new (weak) unspecified-knob axis in
docs/unspecified-choices.md row 10 — **documented, not adopted, spread
unmeasured**; per CLAUDE.md these are the author's example-code defaults for a
different study, not the paper, and importing them because they might move
turnover toward 1.41 would be fitting.

## R2.4 The lineage published the mechanism: Sharpe is flat in k, turnover is not

Nystrup's 2018 DTU thesis (Paper D, "Dynamic allocation or diversification",
J. Portfolio Management), the Bulla-descendant branch of the §3.4 citation
lineage, implements the Bulla median filter as "the median of the most likely
states over the last 21 days", tests exactly two windows, and reports:

> "The Sharpe ratio is almost the same regardless of whether the number of
> days is 5 or 21 (one week or one month), but the annual turnover is much
> higher when fewer days are considered."

That is the mechanism of our §"the rule cannot choose reliably" finding,
published in the lineage six years before the paper: a Sharpe-scored selection
over filter windows is choosing between near-ties, while the turnover
consequences of the choice are large. A CV that maximizes validation Sharpe
over k is therefore structurally unable to pin down the turnover row — which
is what our split-half agreement test measured (17% against 16.7% chance) and
what Table 4's one unreplicable cell shows.

Also from the same thesis: the *other* Nystrup branch decodes with filtered
probabilities and online evidence accumulation (thresholds 0.9998 / Narasimhan
2006), not Viterbi — but that branch is not what the paper describes, see R2.5.

Source: https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/152802375/phd465_Nystrup_P.pdf,
Table 9 and surrounding text, fetched and read 2026-07-30. The candidate pair
{5, 21} is the only published smoothing-window set anywhere in the citation
lineage; note it contains no 0.

## R2.5 The decoding-convention hypothesis is CLOSED at the primary source

The one implementation degree of freedom that could plausibly have moved
turnover materially — decoding by filtered probability instead of Viterbi —
is excluded by the paper's own words:

> [line 382] "On the training window ending at day t, we use the Viterbi algorithm to decode the hidden"

state sequence, extracting the last identified state as the online inferred
regime — exactly our convention. The author's 2023 code (R2.3) does the same,
and the filtered-probability convention documented in the Nystrup branch is a
different lineage the paper did not adopt. No part of the residual may be
attributed to decoding.
