# Cover letter — *Journal of Asset Management*

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"One Risk Measure, Fewer Trades: A
Parsimonious Jump Model for U.S. Downside Protection,"** for publication in the
*Journal of Asset Management*.

**What the paper does.** It reconstructs, on fully public equity and cash-rate
proxies, the market-or-cash economic test of Shu, Yu, and Mulvey (2024), and asks
whether a statistical jump model needs its full three-feature set. The
reconstruction preserves the features that matter for a tradable signal —
past-only estimation, monthly walk-forward selection, a one-day trading delay,
and ten-basis-point one-way costs. The central finding is that a jump model
driven by a single feature, exponentially weighted downside deviation, produces a
markedly cleaner U.S. strategy than the standard three-feature model: a higher
net Sharpe ratio than both buy-and-hold and a Gaussian HMM, a maximum drawdown
matching the HMM, and far lower turnover. A pre-specified loss-scale control shows
the effect is not merely a scale artefact. We are deliberately conservative: the
result is local to the United States, does not hold in Germany or Japan, and is
framed as exploratory development evidence, not a live-performance claim.

**Fit.** The paper speaks directly to the journal's readership on regime-based
asset allocation and to the Shu et al. (2024) article published in this journal;
it is empirical, implementation-aware, and explicit about costs and limitations.

**Companion-manuscript disclosure.** A separate, methodologically distinct
manuscript by the same author — *"When Should a Jump Model Switch?
Evidence-Adaptive Transition Costs with Exact Decoding"* — is being submitted to
*Annals of Operations Research*. The two papers share only the public data set
and a concise, independently worded description of the trading protocol. They do
not share an abstract, a principal figure or table, a theorem, or any substantive
paragraph. The present paper contains **none** of the adaptive-cost derivations
that are the subject of the companion paper; the companion paper does **not**
present the downside-deviation-only result as a contribution. An overlap audit
(identical phrases longer than 12 words) and a claim-to-reference matrix are
available on request and accompany the project's supplementary material.

**Originality and ethics.** The work is original, is not under consideration
elsewhere, and all data are public and cited. The manuscript includes funding,
competing-interest, data-availability, code-availability, author-contribution,
and AI-assistance statements. All references were verified against Crossref.
